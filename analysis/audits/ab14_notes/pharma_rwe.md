## 1. RWE market size (data, analytics, services), 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Paid market-research firms put the "RWE solutions" market at roughly USD 2.0-2.6B for 2024-2025 and ~USD 3.1B for 2026, growing ~16%/yr; these figures conflict with IQVIA's own filings (IQVIA alone booked USD 674M from a subset of its real-world offerings in 2025), so the analyst numbers are best read as a floor for "RWE software + services" and not as total pharma RWE spend (which also includes data licences and internal headcount).

## 2. Bottom-up inputs: buyers, submissions, registered studies, study and licence costs

### Takeaway
Regulator-counted regulatory RWE is small (FDA CDER FY2025: 31 protocols + 10 NDA/BLAs + 6 final reports; CBER FY2025: 8 protocols + 1 report), but it is growing fast (CDER protocols 10 → 11 → 31 over FY2023-25), and the wider pool of RWE studies (EMA ~180 own RWD studies/yr; ~900+ RWE-labelled registrations/yr on ClinicalTrials.gov) is one to two orders of magnitude bigger. No public price for RWE analytics platform licences was found.

### Inferences
- **Bottom-up estimate A — regulator-counted regulatory RWE studies (estimate).** Inputs: FDA FY2025 CDER 31 protocols + 10 NDA/BLAs + 6 final reports, CBER 8 protocols + 0 + 1 = **56 regulator-reported RWE submissions/yr** (primary); add EMA-side sponsor submissions — no annual count found, so assume parity with FDA (assumption, unsourced) → ~110/yr. At an **assumed** USD 25k-100k per study for a pre-specified assumption ledger + bias-analysis record (assumption: priced as a small fraction of a regulatory RWE study; no sourced RWE study price found), revenue pool = 110 × 25k-100k = **USD 2.8M-11M/yr**. This is the "must-have" core, and it is small.
- **Bottom-up estimate B — all registered RWE / observational studies (estimate).** Inputs: ~927 RWE-labelled ClinicalTrials.gov registrations starting in 2022 (second-hand; keyword-based undercount); ~180 EMA RWD studies/yr (~2025, primary); assume EU sponsor-registered studies in the HMA-EMA catalogue are of similar order to the US keyword count (assumption, unsourced) → ~2,000 studies/yr. At an **assumed** USD 10k-50k per study ledger → **USD 20M-100M/yr**.
- **Top-down cross-check (estimate).** If an audit/provenance layer captured 1-3% of the GMI 2025 market (USD 2.6B, second-hand) → **USD 26M-78M/yr**, consistent with estimate B.
- **Seat-based cross-check (estimate).** ISPOR ~18,000 members (primary, undated) as a proxy for HEOR/RWE professionals; assume 30% at sponsors/CROs and 10% of those adopt at USD 10k/seat/yr (all three are assumptions) → 540 seats × 10k = **USD 5.4M/yr**. Shows that a per-seat model is weaker than per-study pricing tied to submissions.
- The 5 externally controlled trial protocols (CDER FY2025) plus 4 ECT-based NDA/BLAs are the highest-value niche: an ECT that replaces part of a comparator arm is worth USD 10-20M per trial (BCG, second-hand), so a ledger that lowers CRL risk could be priced as insurance on a large ticket, not as a small analytics fee.
- The ratio of CRLs (4 CRL + 1 RTF out of 10 RWE NDA/BLAs in FY2025, primary) is the strongest quantitative argument that RWE packages fail at review.

### Gaps
- No public count of sponsors or CROs that run RWE studies (no registry field, no survey with a company count). Deloitte's n=25 and TriNetX's n=150 are sample sizes, not population counts.
- No annual count of studies newly registered in the HMA-EMA RWD catalogue; the catalogue page gave no totals.
- No primary typical cost of an observational/RWE study and no list price for any RWE analytics platform (Aetion, Flatiron, TriNetX, Truveta, Komodo, IQVIA E360) was found; licence pricing is negotiated and private.

### Inferences
- The natural acquirers/partners for an assumption-ledger layer are data networks that already bundle analytics (Datavant-Aetion, TriNetX, Truveta) and CROs that sell regulatory RWE (IQVIA R&DS, ICON, Parexel, Fortrea). Selling standalone against Aetion's "causal inference" platform is the hardest route.
- "Traceable/governed AI" is already marketing language (Komodo Marmot, Atropos "no hallucinations"), so the differentiator must be specific and checkable: a hashed pre-specification record, per-claim source typing (data / prior trial / expert / LLM), and abstention — none of which any vendor page read claims.
- Unlearn/PROCOVA remains the only regulator-qualified ML-in-evidence precedent; its argument (inference valid whatever the ML quality) is the template for the ledger's "never below data-only output" property (per AU5 §What this changes, item 2).

### Gaps
- No public price for any RWE platform or dataset licence (Aetion, Flatiron, TriNetX, Truveta, Komodo, Optum, IQVIA E360). TriNetX pricing pages surfaced only third-party review sites, not opened.
- No dedicated commercial "target-trial-emulation tool" vendor with funding was identified; TTE appears inside Aetion, OHDSI open-source tooling and CRO services (not verified in this round).
- No 2025-2026 funding round found for a startup specifically positioned on causal inference for regulatory RWE.

## 4. Regulatory drivers and their 2025-2026 status

### Takeaway
The regulatory pull strengthened in 2025-2026: ICH M14 reached Step 4 (Sep 2025) and FDA finalised it (4 Mar 2026); FDA finalised a device RWE guidance requiring causal diagrams and time-stamped protocols (Dec 2025); EMA adopted its RWD non-interventional reflection paper (Mar 2025); but the FDA AI credibility guidance is **still a draft** (Jan 2025) and the EU AI Act's high-risk duties are postponed and largely do not cover drug RWE.

### Cited Findings
- **FDA AI credibility guidance** ("Considerations for the Use of AI to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products"): still **Draft Level 1**, published 7 Jan 2025, docket FDA-2024-D-4689, comments accepted any time; page content current as of 6 Jan 2025 — primary — [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-use-artificial-intelligence-support-regulatory-decision-making-drug-and-biological). Agrees with AB13 (read 12 Oct 2026).
- **FDA-EMA "Guiding Principles of Good AI Practice in Drug Development"** (14 Jan 2026): principles only (traceability of data origin, processing and analytical choices; whole-system evaluation including humans) — primary, per AB13 rows 20-21.
- **ICH M14** (general principles for planning, designing, analysing and reporting non-interventional studies using RWD for safety assessment): **Step 4 reached Sep 2025** (RAPS article 15 Sep 2025); protocol must describe data sources and their fitness for use; addresses bias and confounding; multi-source studies must explain data integration — second-hand — [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/ich-adopts-the-m14-guideline-on-observational-stud.html). EMA has published the Step 5 version — primary (PDF not opened) — [EMA M14 Step 5 PDF](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/ich-m14-guideline-general-principles-planning-designing-analysing-reporting-non-interventional-studies-utilise-real-world-data-safety-assessment-medicines-step-5_en.pdf)
- **FDA final M14 guidance:** "Final Level 1 Guidance", published **4 Mar 2026**, docket FDA-2024-D-2754 — primary — [FDA M14 page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/m14-general-principles-planning-designing-analyzing-and-reporting-non-interventional-studies-utilize)
- **FDA device RWE final guidance (18 Dec 2025):** causal diagram in protocol; time-stamped protocol revisions; first instance of data plus audit trail — primary, per AB13 (corrections section and rows 18-19).
- **FDA RWE drug guidances** (final: RWD/RWE data standards and reliability 2023, EHR/claims 2024; drafts: externally controlled trials Feb 2023, non-interventional studies 2024) — statuses per AB13 rows 8-17 (read 12 Oct 2026). A newer FDA draft "Demonstrating Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness" (file dated 29 May 2026) was seen only in search results in AB13 — unread.
- **EMA Reflection Paper on use of RWD in non-interventional studies to generate RWE:** adopted **17 Mar 2025** (RAPS, 8 Apr 2025); new chapter says inadequately controlled bias and confounding "may limit the causal interpretation"; model assumptions, diagnostics and sensitivity analyses belong in the SAP; recommends the HARPER protocol template and a data-quality framework — second-hand — [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/ema-adopts-reflection-paper-on-rwe-from-noninterve.html); EMA PDF (not opened) — [EMA reflection paper PDF](https://www.ema.europa.eu/system/files/documents/other/rwe-reflection-paper-en.pdf)
- **DARWIN EU:** ~40 data partners, ~250M patients, ~110 studies since 2022; coordination centre re-tender (EMA/2025/CPN/0009); EHDS regulation in force March 2025 — primary — [EMA DARWIN EU](https://www.ema.europa.eu/node/70257)
- **EMA AI reflection paper (2024, final):** LLM output needs close human supervision; pivotal-trial ML pipelines frozen in the SAP before database lock — primary, per AB13 rows 22-25.
- **EU AI Act:** Digital Omnibus final approval by Council 29 Jun 2026; high-risk duties from 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I); medicines regulation and scientific research are not Annex III areas; Art. 2(6) research exclusion — primary (Commission AI Act Service Desk), per AB13 §EU AI Act.

### Inferences
- The binding documentation requirements for drug RWE come from FDA/EMA/ICH guidance, not from the EU AI Act. The AI Act matters only if the tool ships inside a medical device (Annex I) — use it as a design target, not a sales driver.
- ICH M14 being final in the US (Mar 2026) and adopted by EMA makes protocol/data-source documentation for safety RWE a global baseline; safety PASS/PMR studies (16 PMR protocols at CDER in FY2025) are the largest regulator-counted category and the most standardised entry point.
- Because the FDA AI credibility guidance is still draft, an "FDA AI credibility report generator" claim must be phrased as aligned-to-draft.

### Gaps
- Whether FDA has finalised the externally controlled trials draft (Feb 2023) or the non-interventional draft (2024) after AB13 was written: not re-checked today; AB13 (12 Oct 2026) lists both as draft.
- EMA's implementation date for ICH M14 and the EMA reflection paper's full PDF not read.

## 5. Buyer, budget line and channel

### Takeaway
The buyer for regulatory-grade RWE is the sponsor's RWE / epidemiology / biostatistics function (with regulatory affairs as approver), increasingly budgeted with clinical development (IQVIA moved Real-World Late Phase into its R&D segment in 2026); HEOR/market-access buys RWE for payers on a separate, larger-volume line. Channels that already exist are data-vendor bundles (Datavant-Aetion), CRO service lines, and strategic investors from pharma and distribution (Merck GHI, Cencora, McKesson in Atropos).

## 6. Demand signals for provenance and audit

### Takeaway
There is concrete public evidence that RWE fails review over bias and pre-specification (Biohaven troriluzole CRL, Nov 2025; 4 CRLs + 1 RTF among FDA's 10 RWE NDA/BLAs in FY2025; EMA judged 33% of RWD external controls "inadequate"), and the HEOR field openly says LLM-assisted evidence has no validation or accountability standard yet — but buyer surveys rank data compatibility/privacy and skills above audit as barriers.

### Cited Findings
- **Biohaven troriluzole (Vyglxia), spinocerebellar ataxia:** CRL received **4 Nov 2025**; Biohaven says FDA "cited issues that can be inherent to real-world evidence and external control studies including potential bias, design flaws, **lack of pre-specification** and unmeasured confounding factors"; earlier FDA minutes (8 Mar 2024) quoted: "a large and robust treatment effect would be needed to overcome the biases of an externally controlled trial" — primary (company press release; CRL itself not public at the time) — [Biohaven IR](https://ir.biohaven.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fda-issues-complete-response-letter-biohavens-vyglxia); STAT coverage (paywalled after intro) — [STAT, 6 Nov 2025](https://www.statnews.com/2025/11/06/fda-biohaven-uniqure-sarepta/)
- FDA FY2025: of 10 NDA/BLAs with RWE, 4 CRLs and 1 refuse-to-file — primary — [FDA CDER](https://www.fda.gov/science-research/real-world-evidence/real-world-evidence-submissions-center-drug-evaluation-and-research)
- EMA RWD external controls (2015-2023, n=21): 33% judged inadequate; criticisms selection bias, non-comparable baselines, unmeasured confounding, inconsistent outcome definitions — second-hand (poster) — [ISPOR Europe 2025](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-europe-2025/poster-session-2-2/real-world-data-for-external-controls-insights-from-ema-regulatory-submissions)
- Prior evidence: RCT-DUPLICATE agreement r = 0.82 overall, 0.93 where closely emulable, 0.53 otherwise — second-hand, per AU5 F6/C31.
- **Industry surveys on barriers:** Deloitte 2025 (n=25): data privacy/security 27%, shortage of RWE+AI expertise 25%, regulatory uncertainty 23%; 80% expect generative AI to significantly reshape evidence generation within 12 months — second-hand — [Becaris](https://becarispublishing.com/digital-content/blog-post/deloitte-s-latest-rwe-benchmarking-survey-highlights-biopharma-s-next-steps-real-world). TriNetX (n=150): top barrier is data compatibility (plus interoperability and security); 100% agree RWE can improve regulatory submissions — vendor claim — [TriNetX](https://trinetx.com/blog/ai-real-world-data-and-inclusion-in-pharma-2025-industry-survey-report/)
- **LLMs in pharma analytics and governance:**
  - ISPOR ELEVATE-GenAI reporting guideline for LLM use in HEOR (ISPOR Working Group on Generative AI; arXiv Jan 2025) — primary (preprint; not opened, title from search) — [arXiv 2501.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12394v1)
  - Komodo (29 May 2026, ISPOR 2026 recap): AI rose from 3rd to 1st in ISPOR's 2026-2027 HEOR Trends report; "no consensus" on validating LLM-assisted evidence — teams build non-portable heuristics that "won't scale or withstand regulatory scrutiny"; the PI-sponsor-reviewer accountability chain blurs because reviewers may not know which steps were human-specified vs model-generated; ELEVATE-GenAI called "a meaningful start" but not operationalised — vendor-authored commentary — [Komodo](https://www.komodohealth.com/perspectives/ai-in-heor-what-ispor-2026-revealed/)
  - ISPOR 2026 poster on secure LLM deployment in HEOR (governance, risk mitigation) and an ISPOR Europe 2026 short course (8 Nov 2026) on LLMs for RWE generation — titles only, not opened — [ISPOR 2026 poster](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-3-3/secure-deployment-of-large-language-models-in-heor-governance-infrastructure-and-risk-mitigation-best-practices); [ISPOR Europe 2026 course](https://www.ispor.org/conferences-education/event/2026/11/08/default-calendar/november-8--practical-applications-of-large-language-models-for-real-world-evidence-generation-and-heor----in-person-at-ispor-europe-2026)
  - Regulators: EMA AI reflection paper (2024) requires close human supervision of generative output; FDA AI draft (2025) asks for context of use, risk = influence × consequence, credibility report; FDA-EMA principles (Jan 2026) ask that data origin, processing and analytical choices be traceable — primary, per AB13 rows 1-7, 20-25.

### Inferences
- The strongest demand signal for an assumption ledger is the CRL pattern for externally controlled / natural-history-comparator submissions: the cited defects (pre-specification, bias, unmeasured confounding) map one-to-one onto ledger fields (plan hash + deviations, bias-analysis record, typed assumptions incl. no-unmeasured-confounding). Pitch: "make the ECT defence reviewable before submission."
- The LLM-governance gap (no validation consensus; reviewers can't tell human-specified from model-generated steps) maps directly onto per-claim source typing (expert vs LLM vs data) — this is the novel wedge versus Aetion-style platforms.
- Surveys do not show "auditability" as a top-3 buyer barrier, so the ledger should be sold as reducing regulatory risk (CRL avoidance) and enabling compliant LLM use, not as "audit" per se. Per AU5, willingness to pay for calibrated confidence/abstention remains an untested hypothesis.
