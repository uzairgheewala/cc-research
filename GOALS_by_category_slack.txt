*5. Where we have to reach (targets)*

Score targets by benchmark and regime:
• Semantic scenarios, numbers only: published best 0.634 (FoundCause), then GIES 0.552, PC 0.333 (ours 0.335). Ceiling 0.957.
• Semantic, numbers + interventions: published best 0.568 (GIES). Ceiling at least 0.974.
• Formula scenarios, numbers only: published best 0.524 (FoundCause). Ceiling 0.971.
• Formula, numbers + interventions: at least 0.534 (TabCausal; check the other rows). Ceiling at least 0.993.
• Real data (13 sets incl. Sachs, Causal Chambers, PetShop): 0.391 observational (CDFM); 0.462 with interventions (TabCausal, 7 sets).
• Causal Chambers alone: 0.727 reported in CDFM's own paper (second-hand; check which subset).
• Numbers + declared knowledge (names read by a model, given as tiers): no published comparator. Our target: at least 0.634 semantic and 0.524 formula, columns shuffled.

What 0.634 from numbers alone requires (derived from the PC budget):
• Directed F1 ≈ skeleton F1 × direction accuracy. PC is about 0.61 × 0.55.
• To reach 0.634, for example: skeleton 0.80 with directions 0.79, or skeleton 0.70 with directions 0.91.
• Even with directions as good as identifiability allows (about 0.96), the skeleton must reach about 0.66. PC's is 0.61.

Targets that are not scores:
• Never below the system's own data-only output.
• Zero change when columns are shuffled.
• Bounded harm when names are wrong.
• Calibration better than raw bootstrap frequencies on held-out scenarios.
• Zero crashes from malformed model output.
• Within the scoreboard's 1-hour budget per graph.
• Predictions of held-out interventions better than regression on all variables.
• Experiment choice better than random on recorded Causal Chambers experiments.

*6. Required capabilities (have vs need)*
Format: capability. Why. Causal-Copilot today / ours today. Status.
• Skeleton recall at variables with many causes. It is the main skeleton loss. CC: independence search (PC/FCI) / ours: joint test per candidate edge, designed. [open]
• Direction from the numbers. Currently 54% under PC. CC: algorithm output, then overwritten by the LLM / ours: none yet. [open]
• Using intervention data. Worth +0.02 to +0.04 for published methods, more in principle. CC: none (CD-NOD via a domain index only) / ours: designed. [open]
• Declared knowledge as tiers, never as edges. The biggest lever measured (0.335 → 0.470 from "which are inputs" alone). CC: names turned into edges, undeclared / ours: designed, plus an elicitation test. [waiting: Uzair]
• Invariance to column order. A leak control. CC: untested / ours: an invariant in the design. [open]
• Keeping "direction unknown" edges. CC deletes them / ours: designed. [open]
• Calibrated confidence per claim. CC: 20-resample frequency of directed edges only; the literature says bootstrap frequencies are often miscalibrated. [open]
• Abstention (claiming less when unsure). CC: none. [open]
• Provenance per edge. CC: none natively, we reconstruct it from outside / ours: claim ledger with an independent checker, designed. [open]
• Robustness to malformed model output. CC: 12 of 84 runs crash / ours: validate, retry once, proceed without. [open]
• Determinism. CC: numpy unseeded, LLM answers vary / ours: same inputs give the same ledger hash. [open]
• Runtime at 20-40 variables. PC takes 0.3 s on a median file, 105 s on the worst; CC reruns its algorithm 20 times and asks the LLM about every pair. [open, to measure]
• Nonlinear and mixed-type dependence. PC's recall ranges from 0.42 (stock-and-flow mechanisms) to 0.65 (linear). [open]
• Hidden common causes (PAG marks). CC's bootstrap step destroys FCI's marks. [open]
• Answering "what if we set X?" with identification status and bounds. CC: effect module not yet examined. [open]
• Choosing the next experiment. None. [open]
• Scoreboard packaging (CPDAG→DAG rule, summary.csv, validator). We have the scorer and the rule. [partial]
• Cost per run. About 50k prompt tokens per run at 5-9 variables. [open, to measure at 25]

*7. Research questions and predictions*
Settled:
• Pass 4b (Sachs): P1, P2, P3 supported.
• Bundle 1: N2 supported; N1, N4, P3 inconclusive.

Theses (program v2):
• T1. Where a method gets its order information explains most of its directed-edge accuracy.
• T2. Deployed agents take order from names and layout, not the numbers.
• T3. A system that keeps order as a typed, sourced partial order is:
    ◦ never below its data-only self;
    ◦ boundedly harmed by wrong knowledge;
    ◦ aiming for the best published score in each declared regime (a target, not a promise).
• T4. Calibrated, machine-checkable claims with abstention are possible.
• T5. Answers to "what if" questions can come with identification status, checked on held-out interventions.
• T6. Experiment choice beats random on recorded real experiments.

Next predictions, O1-O5 (model supplies tiers from names):
• O1. With real names, ≥70% of true edges ordered correctly and ≤10% contradicted.
• O2. Neutral names give no information.
• O3. With moved names, the tiers follow the labels.
• O4. Regression on the model's tiers reaches 0.634 (semantic).
• O5. Same for formula, against 0.524.

Open questions:
• The semantic scenarios were written with LLM help. Does that inflate name-based scores?
• Does PC's error pattern hold for other algorithms?
• Do results hold for a second model?
• Why does SHD not reproduce?
• Do the scoreboard's wrappers shuffle columns?

*9. Evaluation protocol (how we measure)*
• [standing] Every score states its regime: numbers only / + interventions / + declared knowledge / all three.
• [standing] Controls on every score:
    ◦ columns always shuffled;
    ◦ names real / neutral / moved to other columns;
    ◦ knowledge supplier none / oracle / each model;
    ◦ interventions withheld / released / chosen.
• [standing] Metrics:
    ◦ scoreboard F1;
    ◦ skeleton precision and recall;
    ◦ direction accuracy;
    ◦ precision at stated coverage, and calibration error;
    ◦ error on held-out interventions;
    ◦ cost, runtime, crash rate.
• [standing] Predictions and thresholds are fixed in code before every live run, and plan files are hashed.
• [open] Split the public scenarios into development and held-out sets by scenario number, before any tuning.
• [standing] Any pipeline is scored stage by stage (algorithm output → each refinement step → final).
• [standing] Every live step has a must-show list and pass/fail gates.
